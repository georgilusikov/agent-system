# Example: MMCH - Icebreaker Hull Wear

## Input
"The hull of an icebreaker wears out rapidly due to friction against the ice. Making the steel harder is too expensive."

## Execution Trace (Little Men Modeling)

### 1. Population (The Actors)
- **Group A (Hull):** Defensive line of little men. They are standing arm-in-arm, trying to protect the ship.
- **Group B (Ice):** Aggressive little men with knives. They are scratching and attacking Group A.
- **Group C (Water):** Neutral little men floating lazily behind the Ice group.

### 2. The Conflict (Drama)
- Group B attacks Group A. Group A gets tired and injured (wear).
- Direct fight is losing for Group A because Group B is very sharp/hard.

### 3. Solution (Reconfiguration)
- **Idea 1:** Make Group A stronger? (Harder steel - rejected).
- **Idea 2:** Use Group C!
- **Tactic:** Let Group C (Water men) slip **between** Group A and Group B.
- **Action:** Group C forms a "living shield" or "lubricant layer". Group B's knives hit the Water men (who don't care, they are fluid) instead of the Hull men.

### 4. Engineering Translation
- **Concept:** Introduce a water or air layer between hull and ice.
- **Physical Effect:** Air-bubbling system or high-pressure water jets on the hull surface to reduce friction.

## Output
- **Model:** Interposed layer of fluid.
- **Result:** Reduced friction wear. (Standard modern icebreaker design includes bubbling systems).
